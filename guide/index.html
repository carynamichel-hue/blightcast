🍃 BlightCast boxwood blight risk planner

🧭 How to use BlightCast

BlightCast watches accumulated boxwood blight infection risk at your own locations, using the published USPest.org / Oregon State University model (v3.0). Risk builds up as degree-hours (DH) during wet, mild hours and resets after 6 straight dry hours — the 📖 Model tab explains the model itself. This page is about using the app.

1 · Get started

Open ⚙ Setup and type your town into 📍 Locations. That gets you running — then the optional customizations are worth a minute each:

  • 🔔 Alert level — the DH level you want to be warned about. Each town keeps its own. 56 DH (first infection on highly susceptible varieties) is the cautious default.
  • 🌱 What grows here — tailors the guidance text to your planting.
  • 💧 Leaf-wetness estimate — two dials per town; the panel suggests settings for humid vs dry sites.
  • 🧴 My products — save your usual fungicides once (name, label days, FRAC, wash-off) and logging becomes two clicks.

Have more than one growing site? Add each as its own location — a nickname like "Home farm" or "North houses" works (✎ next to the name). Every location keeps its own alert level, cultivar, dials, station links and spray log.

2 · Reading the Risk tab

  • 📍 Locations (with 2+ towns) — one card per town: the action verdict in color, then the current DH, the predicted crossing date, and protection status. Click a card to switch towns; the chips in the white bar switch too.
  • 🍃 Today — the current DH, its risk level, and the one line that matters: whether action is needed, with your spray log taken into account.
  • The chart — the season curve: colored strips are the published risk levels, the dashed blue line is your alert level, 🧴 spans show logged protection (a red cap = rain washed it off early). Hover (tap on phones) for any hour's detail; click to open that day. The dropdown zooms (This week → Next month); ⛶ opens full screen (turns landscape on a phone); 👻 overlays last year's curve for comparison; 📸 saves a shareable picture; ⬇ Export downloads a daily or hourly spreadsheet for any date range.
  • 📅 Day by day — two weeks of tiles (swipe on phones). Click one for its hour-by-hour table, every hour labeled with its weather source.

3 · The spray log

Log each protective spray with the date and how long its label says it protects (the 🧴 button in the white bar jumps straight to the form). The chart shades the protected window and the Today card tells you when to re-cover — including a 📅 calendar reminder you can download (and one per spray for the day its protection runs out). An optional wash-off amount ends protection early once that much rain falls — label days always stay the ceiling. The product box fills details as you type from your saved products. ⬇ Excel exports the whole log.

Turn on 🔔 Alerts (Setup) and BlightCast chimes when a new forecast crossing of any town's alert level appears while the page is open.

4 · Weather sources (all optional)

Out of the box, risk runs on Open-Meteo weather for your exact spot. In ⚙ Setup → 🛰 Weather sources you can do better:

  • 🦅 NWS/NDFD — switch the forecast to the National Weather Service's gridded forecast (US only). The 📋 scorecard below it keeps score of which source has been closer at your town — check it after a couple of weeks before choosing.
  • 🔮 Season outlook — extends the chart months ahead using your spot's 10-year average weather adjusted by NOAA's seasonal model. An outlook, not a forecast: read crossings as season timing, never spray dates.
  • 🛰 NEWA station — link a nearby public station; a real leaf-wetness sensor drives the model directly.
  • 🔌 LI-COR Cloud — connect your own HOBO RX logger with its API token.
  • 📡 CSV import — any station export, past or future hours (a forecast file, like USPest's 10-year-average outlook, imports the same way).

Precedence is simple: your measurements beat any forecast for the hours they cover.

5 · Sharing, backup, phones

  • 🔗 Share setup (Setup → Locations) — copies a link that sets a coworker's browser up with your towns and their settings: alert levels, cultivar, wetness dials, forecast choices and station links. Never your spray log or tokens.
  • 💾 Backup — a file you keep (or hand over); importing previews everything before it replaces anything.
  • 📤 Google Sheet — optional: spray logs from everyone's devices land in one sheet you set up.
  • 📱 On a phone — open the link, then "Add to Home Screen": BlightCast runs as an app. It opens without signal, but the risk picture needs a connection — offline it tells you it couldn't fetch the weather and retries once you're back online.

Questions, ideas, something broken? The 🔒 Privacy statement in ⚙ Setup has the details on what the app does and doesn't send — and Caryn's email at the bottom.
