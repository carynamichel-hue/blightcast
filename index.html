📖 How the model works

Boxwood blight (Calonectria pseudonaviculata) infects when leaves are wet and temperatures are mild. This tool implements the boxwood blight infection risk model v3.0 published by Len Coop at Oregon State University for USPest.org — a "degree-hours during leaf wetness" model:

  • Each wet hour between 44°F and 86°F adds infection risk units (degree-hours, DH) from the model's published temperature table — rising to a peak of 31.35 DH/hour at 76°F, then falling to zero at 86°F.
  • Dry hours add nothing, and 6 straight dry hours end the infection cycle — accumulated DH resets to zero.
  • Without a leaf-wetness sensor, BlightCast estimates wetness each hour from the weather: rain (following the model's precipitation-wetness rules) and humidity/dew (wet when RH reaches your dew threshold — default 90% — in light wind). You can tune the sensitivity in ⚙ Setup. Weather comes from Open-Meteo by default; in ⚙ Setup you can switch the forecast to the 🇺🇸 NWS/NDFD (the National Weather Service's gridded forecast, US only — the 📋 scorecard shows which has been closer at your town), extend the view months ahead with the 🔮 season outlook (10-year averages + NOAA's seasonal model), or bring in real station data (🛰): link a nearby NEWA network station, connect your own station's cloud service (LI-COR Cloud), or import a CSV by hand. Station readings outrank any forecast for the hours they cover, and a real leaf-wetness sensor is used by the model directly.

Less susceptible cultivars run lower risk than shown — set what grows at each location with the 🌱 dropdown in ⚙ Setup and the guidance lines adjust.

⚠ Model assumptions — read once

  • The model assumes inoculum is present and the host is a susceptible, not-yet-protected cultivar. It predicts conditions favorable for infection — not that infection has happened.
  • Lesion estimates are approximate, for gauging severity risk only.
  • Plants under overhead irrigation or heavy shade run higher risk than the model shows (it assumes ambient exposure).
  • In the very humid Southeast, humidity alone can sometimes wet canopies more than models capture — if outbreaks outrun the numbers, lower the dew threshold in ⚙ Setup (e.g. 87%).
  • A forecast is a forecast: the past week shown is actual weather, the next 7 days are the forecast, and numbers can differ from a weather station a few miles away. Scout your plants — the model never replaces monitoring.

🔔 Alert level

Each town keeps its own level — the chart line, the status verdict and the re-spray planner all follow it. 56 DH is the published first-infection threshold for highly susceptible cultivars.

💧 Leaf-wetness estimate (used only for hours without a wetness sensor)

The published model's dew proxy — each town keeps its own, because humidity behaves differently site to site. Starting points:

  • Default (RH 90% · counts from 1) — the published model's behavior. Start here and only move a dial when what you scout disagrees with the numbers.
  • Very humid site → RH 87–88% — the OSU model notes' Southeast caveat: where humid air wets canopies more than models capture (muggy nights, dense canopy, overhead irrigation, poor air movement), lower the threshold so those nights count. 87% is the notes' own example. Use this when blight shows up ahead of the chart.
  • Dry, breezy, exposed site → RH 92–93% — where mornings dry fast and the chart runs hotter than what you scout, a higher threshold stops counting every humid-ish night.
  • "Counts from" (the /10 dial) — at 1, any trace of wetness accrues risk. Raise to 2–3 to ignore the faintest dew film; go higher only if a connected leaf-wetness sensor reads damp when leaves are actually dry.

A real leaf-wetness sensor replaces the RH estimate for the hours it covers — but the "counts from" dial still decides how wet its 0–10 reading must be to count. Move one dial at a time and check the chart against what you actually see on the plants.

🔔 Alerts on this device

When ON, BlightCast chimes and pops a note whenever a new forecast crossing of a town's alert level appears — every town is watched, each at its own level — while the page is open in this browser. Nothing runs when the page is closed.

📤 Spray log → Google Sheet (optional — one shared sheet the whole team reads)

The sheet owner makes a Google Form once (short-answer questions for product · days · frac · location · notes · status · dh, plus a Date-type question), then ⋮ → "Get pre-filled link", types those words into their matching fields as markers, copies the link, and pastes it here. New log entries (and removals) then flow to the sheet automatically. The form must be Published — an unpublished form silently accepts nothing.

The sheet is write-only from here — this app never reads anything back from it, and only spray fields are sent (never towns, weather, or station tokens).

🔒 Privacy statement

There is no account and no BlightCast server: your locations, settings, spray log and any weather file you import are stored only in this browser on this device (imported files are read right here in the browser — the file itself is not sent anywhere).

What does leave the browser: weather requests, carrying your town's coordinates and nothing else — to Open-Meteo (hourly weather, town search, the 👻 last-year archive and the 🔮 seasonal feed), and, only for sources you turn on in ⚙ Setup: NWS/weather.gov (the 🇺🇸 forecast and the 📋 scorecard's observation checks), NEWA's public station service at Cornell, and LI-COR Cloud — your token goes only to api.licor.cloud and is stored only in this browser, never in backups or share links.

If you connect the optional 📤 sheet bridge, spray-log rows are written to the Google Form/Sheet you set up — that is the one place your spray log can go, and only because you connected it. 🔗 setup share-links carry towns, alert levels, wetness dials and station links — never the spray log, tokens or feed addresses. 💾 backups are files you keep and hand to whoever you choose.

A model is a model: BlightCast is planning help, not a diagnosis. It flags conditions favorable for infection assuming the pathogen is around — scouting and the product label always win. See the 📖 Model tab for assumptions and credits.
